# Wine appreciation example domain pack
Scope: general adult wine education, sensory literacy, geography, vinification, service and pairing, with optional alcohol-free exercises.
Illustrative five categories: still red, still white, rosé, sparkling, fortified; these categories overlap (e.g., sparkling rosé), so do not imply a mutually exclusive taxonomy.
Wine acidity creates a mouthwatering sensation; tannin commonly creates drying/astringency. A wine's colour alone cannot diagnose quality, age or grape conclusively.
Primary aroma often relates to grape/fermentation-derived fruit/floral characteristics; secondary from vinification; tertiary from maturation. Boundaries are interpretative, not hard chemical categories.
Cork taint may suppress fruit aromas and contribute mouldy/cardboard notes, but "musty" alone is not definitive laboratory diagnosis.
Classic examples to compare: Cabernet Sauvignon (often fuller-bodied and higher tannin) vs Pinot Noir (often lighter tannin) with producer, climate and technique exceptions.
Food pairing: consider heat, sweetness, acidity, fat, umami, tannin and alcohol; no universal "correct" pairing. Optional exercise: compare flavours through aroma jars, grapes, tea tannin and citrus acidity without alcohol.
Suggested initial resources: Karen MacNeil, *The Wine Bible* (check edition); Hugh Johnson and Jancis Robinson, *The World Atlas of Wine* (check edition); Wine & Spirit Education Trust introductory materials.
Safety: no consumption exercises for minors; offer abstinent alternatives for every practical task.
